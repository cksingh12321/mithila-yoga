Our Mission

To honour, study, and share the great yoga tradition of Mithila.

Mithila Yoga is an independent institution dedicated to the contemplative heritage of north Bihar — a land whose sages, kings, and householders gave the world some of its earliest enquiries into consciousness, breath, and self. We are a retreat sanctuary for visiting teachers, a growing archive for scholars, and a public museum for anyone curious about where yoga was first thought into being.

The Heritage

A land that thought yoga into being.

Long before yoga became a global practice, Mithila — the ancient kingdom of Videha — was a crucible of philosophical enquiry. Its court was a meeting ground for sages, its forests for seekers, its rivers for ritual. Much of what later traditions inherited was first articulated here.

King Janaka

Philosopher-king of Videha

A monarch who was also a realised teacher — a household yogi whose dialogues with sages appear across the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad. His court at Mithila was the great Upanishadic salon of its age.

Sage Yājñavalkya

Architect of early Vedānta

The sage of Mithila whose teachings on the Self (ātman), on the nature of the witness, and on the practice of inwardness laid the philosophical ground on which classical yoga would later stand.

Gārgī Vācaknavī

Philosopher, debater

The woman philosopher of Janaka's assembly who questioned Yājñavalkya on the ground of all being. Mithila's yoga lineage has, from its earliest moment, included women as full participants in enquiry.

Sītā

Daughter of Mithila

Born of this very earth — the patient strength of Sītā, daughter of Janaka, threads through Mithila's living spiritual imagination, in song, ritual, and the folk paintings of every village.

01 · The Retreat Centre

A sanctuary for visiting teachers.

The Mithila Yoga Retreat Centre is offered to yoga teachers, meditation guides, and contemplative practitioners who wish to bring their students into the land where the tradition was first articulated.

We do not run retreats ourselves. Instead we hold the place — the shala, the grounds, the quiet — for teachers from anywhere in the world who lead their own programmes here.

02 · The Archives

What Mithila remembers, written down.

The Mithila Yoga Archives is a slowly growing collection devoted to the contemplative life of the Maithil region — its yogic, philosophical, ritual, and folk traditions. Some of what we hold is old; much is in the process of being gathered before it is lost.

Working themes

  • Texts. Editions and translations of relevant Upanishadic, Vedāntic, and tantric sources connected to the region.
  • Oral histories. Interviews with householder yogis, ritualists, and Maithil teachers, recorded in audio and video.
  • Local lineages. Documentation of teacher-student lineages, particularly those rooted in north Bihar villages.
  • Ritual practice. Photographs, recordings, and field notes on the living rituals — kosi, kohbar, vidyapati gosthi, and more — through which Maithil philosophy survives.
  • Art & iconography. Cataloguing of yogic and devotional motifs in Mithila (Madhubani) painting traditions.

Scholars, students, and serious researchers are welcome to write to us about access. We are happy to host visits — please plan in advance, as the archive is small and supervised.

03 · The Museum

A public room for a quiet tradition.

The Mithila Yoga Museum is open to the public — a small, careful space that tells the story of yoga in this region for anyone who walks in. Through objects, image, sound, and word, we try to make audible a tradition that has lived more often in voice than in display.
