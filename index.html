Our Mission

To honour, study, and share the great yoga tradition of Mithila.

Mithila Yoga is an independent institution dedicated to the contemplative heritage of north Bihar — a land whose sages, kings, and householders gave the world some of its earliest enquiries into consciousness, breath, and self. We are a retreat sanctuary for visiting teachers, a growing archive for scholars, and a public museum for anyone curious about where yoga was first thought into being.

The Heritage

A land that thought yoga into being.

Long before yoga became a global practice, Mithila — the ancient kingdom of Videha — was a crucible of philosophical enquiry. Its court was a meeting ground for sages, its forests for seekers, its rivers for ritual. Much of what later traditions inherited was first articulated here.

King Janaka

Philosopher-king of Videha

A monarch who was also a realised teacher — a household yogi whose dialogues with sages appear across the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad. His court at Mithila was the great Upanishadic salon of its age.

Sage Yājñavalkya

Architect of early Vedānta

The sage of Mithila whose teachings on the Self (ātman), on the nature of the witness, and on the practice of inwardness laid the philosophical ground on which classical yoga would later stand.

Gārgī Vācaknavī

Philosopher, debater

The woman philosopher of Janaka's assembly who questioned Yājñavalkya on the ground of all being. Mithila's yoga lineage has, from its earliest moment, included women as full participants in enquiry.

Sītā

Daughter of Mithila

Born of this very earth — the patient strength of Sītā, daughter of Janaka, threads through Mithila's living spiritual imagination, in song, ritual, and the folk paintings of every village.
