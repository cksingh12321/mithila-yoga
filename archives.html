02 · The Archives

What Mithila remembers, written down.

The Mithila Yoga Archives is a slowly growing collection devoted to the contemplative life of the Maithil region — its yogic, philosophical, ritual, and folk traditions. Some of what we hold is old; much is in the process of being gathered before it is lost.

For Scholars A working archive — by appointment.

What we hold

Five working themes.

  • Texts. Editions and translations of relevant Upanishadic, Vedāntic, and tantric sources connected to the region — including Yājñavalkya's dialogues, the Aṣṭāvakra Gītā, Vācaspati Miśra's commentaries, and Vidyāpati's vernacular work.
  • Oral histories. Interviews with householder yogis, ritualists, and Maithil teachers, recorded in audio and video.
  • Local lineages. Documentation of teacher-student lineages, particularly those rooted in north Bihar villages — pandit traditions, mantra-vidyā lines, and family transmissions.
  • Ritual practice. Photographs, recordings, and field notes on the living rituals — Chhath, Vivāha Pañcamī, kosi, kohbar, Vidyāpati Gosthi, and more — through which Maithil philosophy survives.
  • Art & iconography. Cataloguing of yogic and devotional motifs in Mithila (Madhubani) painting traditions.

For scholars

How to request access.

The archive is small and supervised. We are happy to host scholars, postgraduate students, journalists, and serious independent researchers. Please write to us in advance with: your research question, your institutional affiliation if any, the dates you'd like to come, and how long you'd like to spend.

We reserve the right to ask for references. Field notes, photographs, and audio interviews that we record can be shared on terms agreed in advance. We ask that anything published from the archive credit it appropriately.

A note on the work

Why this matters now.

A generation ago, every Maithil village had at least one pandit — a Sanskrit scholar who taught the children, recited the texts at funerals and weddings, kept the panchang, and could be relied on for an argument about Nyāya at any hour. That figure is now rarer. Songs that were learned in childhood are no longer being passed down. Manuscripts in family collections are being lost to silverfish and the monsoon.

The Archives is, in part, an attempt to slow this loss — to record, to translate, to digitise, and to make available. We can't do everything. We hope to do something.
